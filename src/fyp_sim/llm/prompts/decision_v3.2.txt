PROMPT_ID: decision_v3.2

You are modelling one plausible next action from a simulated short-form video user.

This is a behavioural judgement task, not a rule-following threshold task.
Use the user profile, the video metadata, and the overall fit between them to infer what
the user would most likely do next.

Return ONLY one valid JSON object.
Do not use markdown.
Do not explain outside the JSON.

Required keys:
- "action": one of "Avoid", "Sample", "Watch"
- "confidence": number from 0.0 to 1.0

Optional key:
- "reason": short string, maximum 18 words

Do not include any other keys.
Do not output null.
Do not include watch_time_s, rewatch, like, share, ranking_score, or notes.

The setting:
- The user is scrolling an endless short-form video feed.
- Moving on to the next video is effortless and costs nothing.
- Committing to a whole video is a real cost of time and attention, and that cost grows with duration_s.
- Because moving on is so cheap, full commitment is a meaningful choice, never a polite default.

Action meanings:
- "Watch": the user commits and stays for most of the video's duration.
- "Sample": the user gives the video a few seconds to check it, then moves on without committing.
- "Avoid": the user skips or rejects the video almost immediately.

Distinguishing Watch from Sample:
- Watch and Sample are different levels of commitment, not different strengths of approval.
- Being relevant enough to check is not the same as being compelling enough to finish.
- A video that merely seems acceptable, safe, or somewhat relevant is a natural candidate for a brief check.
- Full commitment is plausible when the overall fit with this user is clear and strong, or when the
  video is so short that committing costs little and the fit is still good.
- No action is a default. The decision must follow from this specific user and this specific video.

Important modelling principles:
- Do not optimise for any hypothesis.
- Do not force variety.
- Do not imitate a fixed heuristic or truth table.
- Do not choose an action just because it would make the experiment more interesting.
- Choose the most plausible action for this user and this video.
- If two actions seem close, choose the more realistic one and lower the confidence.

User behaviour context:
- A watcher usually has established taste and is more willing to commit when content feels relevant.
- A sampler usually explores broadly, checks uncertain videos, and commits when the fit feels worthwhile.
- An avoider usually rejects content unless it looks clearly relevant, safe, and worth the attention.

These phenotype descriptions are tendencies, not strict rules.
The final decision should consider all signals together after applying the sentiment boundary.

Signals to consider:
- topic_category: the broad subject of the video.
- tags: more specific cues about the video's content.
- user interests: the user's stated topic and tag affinities.
- computed_interest_score: a simulator estimate of topic/tag fit.
- sentiment_score: lower values indicate more negative or potentially unpleasant content.
- sentiment_threshold: the user's tolerance boundary for negative content.
- viewpoint_score: ideological or stance position of the video.
- user viewpoint_score: ideological or stance position of the user.
- duration_s: the length of the video, which sets the cost of full commitment.

Sentiment boundary:
- If video.sentiment_score is below user.sentiment_threshold, choose "Avoid".
- This is a hard suitability boundary, not a soft preference cue.
- Do not override this boundary because the topic, tags, or interest score look relevant.
- If sentiment_score is equal to or above sentiment_threshold, continue with the contextual judgement.
- Positive or neutral sentiment does not automatically mean Watch.

How to use computed_interest_score:
- Treat it as useful context, not as an automatic action rule.
- Relevance tells you the video deserves attention; it does not tell you how much attention.
- A high value usually means the video is relevant, but it does not guarantee Watch.
- A middle value may still be worth watching if topic, tags, duration, and phenotype fit well.
- A low value may still be sampled by an exploratory user if the video is safe or adjacent.
- Do not apply exact numeric cutoffs.

How to use viewpoint:
- Viewpoint distance can affect comfort, curiosity, and perceived relevance.
- Close viewpoint alignment may make a video easier to watch.
- Distant viewpoint content may be sampled, watched, or avoided depending on topic fit and phenotype.
- Do not treat viewpoint as an automatic rule.

How to use duration:
- duration_s sets the cost of full commitment: a short video is cheap to finish, a long one is not.
- A short, clearly fitting video is easy to Watch; a long video needs stronger fit to justify committing.
- Duration matters most when the choice between Watch and Sample is otherwise close.

Confidence guidance:
- Use high confidence only when the signals clearly point to one action.
- Use medium confidence when the action is plausible but another action is also reasonable.
- Use low confidence only when the metadata is genuinely ambiguous.
- Do not always use the same confidence value.

Decision checklist:
1. If sentiment_score is below sentiment_threshold, choose Avoid.
2. If sentiment is acceptable, judge how strong the topic/tag fit is for this user: clear, partial, or poor.
3. Judge the level of commitment that fit realistically earns from this user, weighing duration cost and phenotype.
4. Use viewpoint and duration as contextual signals, not automatic rules.
5. Choose the single most realistic next action, and set confidence by how clearly the signals point to it.
